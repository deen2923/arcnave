1Summary

ARCNAVE is a free public web tool that turns NASA SPHEREx's all-sky infrared survey into something anyone can watch. Type any sky position or a name: ARCNAVE fetches every real SPHEREx image of that spot, lines the images up on Gaia stars and plays the passes like a short film, so what moves or changes stands out. Movers are circled pass by pass, measured, and checked against ESA Gaia, NASA WISE and the known asteroids. Nothing is ever called a discovery.

Results on NASA's targets (measured by ARCNAVE on real SPHEREx images).
  • Barnard's Star: 9.7″ north in 359 days (9.9″ a year); the catalogue predicts 10.2″.
  • Epsilon Indi B: 4.3 ± 0.3″ a year south-east over 4 passes (5.9″ in 515 days); catalogue 4.7″.
  • WISE 0855: 8.82 ± 1.10″ a year west, AB 17.15 at 11.8σ; CatWISE2020 gives 7.94″, which agrees.
  • RW Aurigae: faded 0.74 mag from Sep 2025 to Sep 2026 in 6 of 6 colours (comparison stars 0.13 mag).
  • R Draconis: changed 1.24 mag at 0.85 µm in 10 images (comparison stars 0.12-0.13 mag).

Everything runs in the visitor's browser and a small Node.js server with no dependencies. The code reads only the rows of each 65 MB SPHEREx image that the field needs (about 4 MB), keeps what it read, and tells the user how sure it is.

4How ARCNAVE uses the data

1 · FindIRSA TAP lists every SPHEREx image covering the spot, all releases
2 · ReadOnly the field's rows of each image from AWS S3: ~4 MB, not 65 MB
3 · CleanFLAGS bits out (saturation, ghosts, halos, cosmic rays); ZODI light removed
4 · MatchWCS-WAVE: images of the same colour in every pass
5 · AlignOn Gaia DR3 stars moved to each image's date
6 · MeasurePSF fits per image; motion through the passes; 625-trial scan
7 · CheckGaia, CatWISE, SkyBoT/MPC/Horizons; "How sure are we?"

The measurements

  • Motion: each star is fitted in every image with an empirical PSF made from that image's own stars (checked against the official SPHEREx PSF); a straight line through the passes gives the motion; a field error floor comes from ~40 steady Gaia stars.
  • Searches: 🔍 bright moving stars, 🔭 deep search on every source of the full stack, ◐ difference search, 🎯 a trial-motion scan of 625 motions for faint sources (shift-and-stack), each with checks (not hopping between stars, steady brightness, Gaia agrees, plausible speed, a second opinion).
  • Brightness: light curves with three steady comparison stars; glare checks for saturated stars.
  • Spectra: ~100 colours from 0.75 to 5 µm per pass by aperture photometry, compared with 2MASS and AllWISE.
  • Known objects: SkyBoT (with the Minor Planet Center as fallback) and JPL Horizons orbits; every asteroid gets a proof card and its track.

Releases

NASA released SPHEREx Data Release 1 in November 2026. ARCNAVE's search lists it in every new spot with no code change. The 5 targets and 18 test fields keep the QR2 + QR3 lists their quoted numbers were measured on, ship with the site, and open without asking the archive; "Check for new data" shows what IRSA lists now and can switch a spot to DR1.

7How far to trust it

Four tests on the same 18 sky fields, picked on a grid before the runs (along the ecliptic every 60°, on it and 40° above and below). Fields where ARCNAVE does badly are in the tables too.

TestWhat it doesResult
Hide Planet X (scorecard)19,880 fake planets hidden in the real images, then searched for, with and without a motion-following stackPlanet at 1000 AU found in 18 of 18 fields (median faintest V 17); at 300 AU in 12 of 18
False alarmsTwo stacks of the same passes that differ in nothing real: every change reported is falseAway from the Milky Way: 22 false alarms in 32 comparisons, 7 of 12 fields with none. In the plane: many more (crowded), and the app warns
BrightnessStars made brighter by known amounts1.5× brighter caught in 82% of 720 stars; 2× in 89%
MoversFake movers of known speed and brightness injected and measuredRecovery by speed and signal reported cell by cell

Every result card has a "How sure are we?" note, an "Honest limits" section names what SPHEREx cannot see, Planet X is always called hypothetical, and candidates are always "unconfirmed".

8Speed and engineering

  • Only the bytes needed: FITS images are stored row by row, so ARCNAVE reads just the field's rows from S3; the smooth ZODI model is rebuilt from 9 rows (7.5 → 5.1 MB per image).
  • Quick first report: a new field reports from 3 images per pass first, then deepens to 8 by itself (about 40 s here, network-bound).
  • Saved for good: archive and catalogue answers and images are kept on disk; the start screen's fields are prewarmed and pinned; the 5 targets and 18 test fields ship with the site.
  • Frozen test fields: after DR1 renamed every file, the test fields keep their measured QR2 + QR3 lists, so they stay instant and their numbers stay true (37 of 37 checked on a fresh server).
  • Responsive page: star shapes built in workers, long searches pause every 50 ms; measured old → new: worst freeze 7.2 → 2.6 s (v0.57.5), panels scroll at once (v0.57.8). Every number unchanged to the last digit.
  • Static files gzipped once with ETags: app.js 56 → 2 ms; a returning visitor downloads nothing (304).
  • Robust: mirrors and fallbacks for every outside service (IRSA → VizieR → MAST for Gaia; SkyBoT → MPC); works offline from its cache; 15+ unit test files, real-data tests and headless browser tests.

11Honest limits and next steps

  • SPHEREx pixels are 6.15″: motions under about a pixel between passes are hard to see; most stars move 0.1-0.3″ a year.
  • Bright stars are saturated (measured from their glow); crowded Milky Way fields give many more false alarms.
  • A new field depends on NASA's archive search (seconds to minutes) and the visitor's internet.
  • QR2 files stay on NASA's S3 until February 2027: the test fields must move to DR1 and be re-measured before then.
  • NEOSSat pictures are shown, not measured (no sky coordinates on the raw frames).

Next

  • Re-measure everything on DR1 (v0.58) and publish the comparison.
  • Use DR1's official ePSF and VARIANCE extension for errors; name STREAK and CROSSTALK in more places.
  • A volunteer mode: many people checking candidates on the same field, with agreement counts.
