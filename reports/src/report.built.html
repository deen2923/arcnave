1Summary

ARCNAVE is a free public web tool. Type a place in the sky (RA and Dec, or a name). ARCNAVE fetches every real NASA SPHEREx image of that spot, lines them up, and plays the sky as a film. Stars that move get a spotlight and a measured track. Every number is checked against ESA Gaia and NASA WISE.

All from real SPHEREx images, in the browser, in seconds.

Honesty is built in. Planet X is always called hypothetical. Nothing is ever called a discovery. Each mover is labelled known, target, candidate, fixed, differs or predicted, and the tool says what it cannot see. Tests were run on 18 fields chosen before the runs, and fields where ARCNAVE does badly are reported too.

2What NASA asked for

Since 2025, NASA's SPHEREx has mapped the whole sky every six months in 102 near-infrared colours (0.75–5 µm). Comets, asteroids, nearby stars and brown dwarfs show themselves by shifting between images, the way Pluto was found in 1930. The challenge asks for a public-facing web tool that shows SPHEREx images of any patch of sky like a little video, so anyone can quickly see how the sky changes.

3How ARCNAVE works

A small Node.js server (no extra packages) talks to the archives and keeps a cache. All the image science runs in the visitor's browser, in background workers. Five "agents" do the work in order, and the page shows each step as it happens.

1 · NavigatorReads the place: RA/Dec, a name, or a NASA target. Draws the whole-sky map and flies there.
2 · ArchivistAsks NASA/IPAC IRSA for every SPHEREx image (QR2 + QR3) and picks colour-matched images in each six-month pass.
3 · AlignerCuts out the patch, removes the zodiacal glow, masks flagged pixels, and lines every image up on Gaia stars.
4 · AnalystStacks each pass, compares passes, finds what changed, and checks known asteroids (SkyBoT, MPC).
5 · ReporterWrites a plain-language card with honest labels; the film, searches and charts open from it.

Key ideas

  • Passes, not single images. SPHEREx sees each spot in about 3–4 passes six months apart, with many images per pass. ARCNAVE takes the median of up to 8 colour-matched images per pass, which lowers the noise and removes cosmic rays.
  • Lined up on Gaia. Every image is registered to up to 60 Gaia DR3 stars. Motions are then measured with a point-spread-function fit, with parallax removed and a χ² check.
  • Two opinions for every mover. A motion found in SPHEREx is compared with Gaia or CatWISE2020. It is labelled agrees, differs or unchecked, never more.
  • Sharable by link. The address holds the view (#ra=…&dec=…&fov=…, #ct=barnard), and ?embed=1 gives a clean embeddable flip-book.

6Moving objects: film, spotlight, searches

6.1 The film with a spotlight

🎬 Animate the movers plays the passes in date order, like Tombaugh's blink comparator.

  • Each moving star gets a spotlight, a glowing track, and a numbered circle (P1, P2…) in every pass.
  • The place it had in the pass before stays as a dated ghost circle: "was here 2025-09-14".
  • A callout gives the true shift between dates, e.g. "moved 15.8″ toward the north-east in 178 days".
  • A solid track is measured on the SPHEREx images. A dashed track is a Gaia/WISE prediction for something too faint or too slow to measure here.

6.2 Four searches for moving stars in any field

SearchWhat it doesBest for
🔍 Find moving starsMeasures every detected star in each pass with a PSF fit, lines it up on Gaia, and fits a straight motion with error bars and a χ² checkBright and medium stars
🔭 Deep searchStacks all images for depth, then follows faint sources pass by pass; a duplicate and position-consistency check keeps noise outFaint movers
◐ Slow moversKernel-matched difference images between passes, with a dipole fit (a star that shifted leaves +/− pairs)Motions under a pixel
🎯 Motion scanAdds up all images along 625 trial motions per source (shift-and-stack, as in KBMOD), at 1/8-pixel stepsVery faint movers

Every find gets a second opinion: a frame-by-frame fit on every single image, lined up on Gaia, with parallax and a per-field error floor. Finds near a saturated star's glare are left out. A star that Gaia says is fixed never enters the film.

6.3 Changes between passes

Changes marks every spot that differs between two passes and sorts it into plain kinds: a known asteroid passing by, a star that got brighter, or "not explained yet". Detector glitches are counted but hidden. Known asteroids are ringed and named from SkyBoT and the Minor Planet Center. Unknown dots are shown but never linked to a name.

7102 colours: composite, spectra, light curves

  • Colour slider: pick any SPHEREx wavelength from 0.75 to 5 µm. Images of matching colour are used in every pass.
  • Deep view per detector: adds up all images from each of the six detector arrays, as NASA suggested. Noise drops by a factor of 1.6–3.5 per detector (Barnard field, 36 images).
  • Colour composite on the sky: D1–2 blue, D3–4 green, D5–6 red, with a legend. Cold brown dwarfs stand out by colour.
  • Spectrum on the fly: tap any star to get its SPHEREx spectrum from every image that covers it. You can compare passes and download a CSV.
  • Light curve: brightness over time with error bars and 3 steady comparison stars, plus a χ² verdict ("varies at Nσ" or "steady").

8Other telescopes, data quality, releases

Overlays

  • Gaia DR3: every star moved to the date of the pass on screen with its own motion; "Measure its motion" compares it with SPHEREx.
  • WISE / CatWISE2020: orange diamonds with arrows for movers of 1″/yr or more, pink for very red sources (cool brown dwarfs, quasars).
  • Known asteroids: SkyBoT and MPC, with track arrows and JPL Horizons tracks.
  • Aladin Lite: 2MASS or DSS sky for context, synced to the view.

Data quality

  • Flags: pixels the SPHEREx pipeline flagged, with counts (orange = bad, left out; violet = outlier only).
  • A mover card warns when its position has flagged pixels in some passes.
  • A Milky Way warning appears where crowding raises false alarms.

Share and report

  • Share link and ?embed=1 iframe code

  • Spectrum and light-curve CSV; report JSON
  • Check it: SIMBAD, Legacy Surveys, MPC checker
  • Send this candidate / Copy report: always says "unconfirmed, not a discovery claim"; ARCNAVE itself sends nothing
  • Copy Python: Astropy + astroquery code that repeats the query

9Planet X and the agents

Planet X is hypothetical, and ARCNAVE says so on every screen. What ARCNAVE can do is measure how faint a distant planet could be and still be caught in a given field.

  • Hide Planet X: hides a fake planet with the right motion and PSF in the real images, and lets the agents look for it.
  • Motion-following stack: shifts the images along a planet's path before stacking, so a close planet that moves within one pass is not lost.
  • Map this field: shows the detection limit against distance.
  • Mover test: adds 32 fake moving stars of known speed and brightness, measures them, then restores the images exactly (checked by checksum).

  • Light server: Node.js with no npm packages. It proxies and caches IRSA, Gaia, CatWISE, SkyBoT and MPC with concurrency limits, retries and stale-while-revalidate. The cache is 8 GB, and the start screen's 30 places are prewarmed and pinned.
  • Science in the browser: FITS reading, WCS with SIP distortion, PSF fitting and median stacks run in Web Workers. Stacks became 8–15× faster in v0.52 with bit-identical results.
  • Ready for new data: the archive is asked live, so new SPHEREx releases appear without code changes. "Check for new data" re-asks IRSA.
  • Ready to deploy: Dockerfile, Caddy HTTPS, and a step-by-step guide for a free, always-on server in US East, next to NASA's SPHEREx copy on AWS S3. There is also an offline zip with portable Node for any Windows laptop.
  • Version control: git history for v0.55 onwards. Every change is listed in the changelog with what was measured.

14Honest limits and next steps

What ARCNAVE cannot do (and says so)

  • Slow stars: most stars move far under 1″ a year. A SPHEREx pixel is 6.2″, so over 1–1.5 years they cannot be measured. In such fields the film says so and shows Gaia's predictions as dashed tracks.
  • Asteroids: they cross a field in hours to days, so they cannot be followed between passes six months apart. They are found as changes and named from SkyBoT and MPC instead.
  • Very faint movers: at S/N 3 per image they are not found; 1″/yr is below the limit over ~400 days.
  • Bright stars saturate: their positions come from their glow, with larger errors, and the cards say so.
  • Crowded Milky Way fields: false alarms rise there, and ARCNAVE warns.
  • One pass is not enough: a spot with only one pass cannot show motion.
  • No discoveries are claimed. Candidates are "unconfirmed" until checked with other data.

Next steps

  • Put the public site on an always-on server (deploy guide ready: Oracle Cloud Always Free, US East).
  • Add new SPHEREx releases as they arrive (no code change needed). More passes mean longer baselines and slower movers in reach.
  • Fit the official SPHEREx PSF on native pixels for better positions of ordinary stars.
  • A volunteer review queue, so candidates can be checked by more than one person.
